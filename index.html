Joined 2019 as Senior Software Developer

The whole environment is provisioned in Bicep. It started on Kubernetes and moved to Azure Container Apps once the workloads proved not to need an orchestrator of that size. It runs in Azure or fully containerized on-premises for data-sensitive deployments, is in daily use by our own teams, and is live with a partner organization ahead of general release.

  • Trained and deployed a Detectron2 Mask R-CNN instance-segmentation model for automated wound boundary detection, serving predictions through a containerized Flask/Gunicorn inference API in Azure Container Registry, deployed to Kubernetes in Azure AI Foundry, with a semi-automated workflow that pulls each capture, normalizes it, and produces a first-pass annotation to speed dataset labeling.
  • Built a document processing system (.NET 10, Semantic Kernel, multi-stage LLM/computer-vision classification) that automates OCR, categorization, and demographic data extraction from 200–300 pieces of daily consumer mail for a debt collection agency. Per-document review time fell 80–90%. It runs entirely on-premises on a Proxmox host with Nvidia RTX PRO 6000 GPUs, with no path to the public internet, behind an ASP.NET Core review UI.
  • Reverse-engineered 25+ years of undocumented insurance business logic from a legacy Visual FoxPro platform (~1.2M LOC, 350 tables) using claims and billing data alone, writing ~22,000 lines of forensic SQL to reconcile legacy vs. modern invoices line-by-line and reconstruct tax, fee, stop-loss, and eligibility rules. The line-by-line comparison surfaced ledger discrepancies that were a regulatory red flag in their own right. Fed those findings into a .NET 6 / Azure rebuild for a health and dental third-party administrator (TPA).
  • Moved that system to domain-driven design (DDD): convinced leadership it was the right direction, then planned the slicing so later slices could land without recreating a big-ball-of-mud. Billing came first: the insurer was losing roughly a day every week to reconciliation between disparate legacy systems, with manual rollback and reapplication behind it, making billing both the largest reliability drain and the largest source of manual error.

About me

The interesting problems arrive with constraints attached. A debt collection agency's incoming mail, a health and dental administrator's claims, a clinic's wound images: where the data goes and who is allowed to read it sits on the same line as throughput and uptime. I build those systems to run in Azure or entirely on hardware the client owns, and I work the same way at home, with models and agents on my own machines, because owning the stack is the only way to learn what it really costs and where it leaks.

This instinct goes back to the bicycle I built to tour Germany on between school and my first full-time job. Assembled from a sturdy frame, every part chosen and bought individually, the wheels laced by hand instead of bought as a set. I wanted to know the machine and how it went together, because a breakdown a long way from a shop looks different once you understand the build. It is the same instinct that had me reconstruct twenty-five years of undocumented insurance logic from claims and billing data alone. I would rather know how a thing actually works than guess.

Off the clock I am a volunteer firefighter, wildland-trained, which is what the Ozarks ask of a department. Wildland work is judgment under pressure, inside a team, with no ambiguity about the outcome. You read the ground and the weather before you commit, then hold your lane. If something looks wrong you say it early, while saying it is still cheap. My own forest asks the same of me when I fell trees. Especially the nerve-wracking jobs near the powerline, read where the tree wants to lean, set the bullrope so it comes down where you decided and nowhere else. Concentrated, safety-first work, most of it about knowing your limits and calling for someone with more experience when a job exceeds them.
